# ELE Practice Analytics — Founding Client Program

**Purpose:** Convert your first denial reviews into 3–5 case studies and
testimonials. You have no proof yet; this program manufactures it fast. Treat the
free work as a marketing cost — every founding client becomes the credibility that
closes the next paying client.

---

## The offer (what you say)

> **Founding Client Program — first 5 Nevada practices only.**
> A full **Denial Diagnostic** — normally a paid engagement — at no cost, in
> exchange for your honest feedback and permission to share the results
> (anonymized if you prefer). You get the complete review and action plan; I get a
> reference I can point the next practice to. Fair trade, one DC to another.

**Why "first 5" and "founding":** scarcity makes it move, and "founding client"
is a status they keep even after you charge full price — worth more to them than
"free."

---

## What each side gets

**The practice gets:**
- A full Denial Diagnostic: denials, A/R aging, and audit-risk baseline
- Plain-language findings + a ranked, dollar-weighted fix list the front desk can
  act on this week
- A 20–30 minute walkthrough call
- Founding-client pricing on any future ongoing work (lock a standing discount)

**You get (the actual point):**
- A written testimonial (see language below)
- Permission to publish an anonymized result ("recovered $X in denied 98940–43
  claims for a Reno practice")
- A named reference for 1–2 future prospects, if they'll allow it
- A repeatable delivery you can now sell with a straight face

---

## Eligibility (screen before you say yes)

Say yes only if the practice is:
1. **Insurance-billing** (not cash-only — cash practices aren't your buyer)
2. **In Nevada**
3. Willing to **send a standard billing export** and **give feedback + a testimonial**
4. One of your **first 5** — after that, the diagnostic is paid

If they're cash-only or won't give feedback, politely pass. Free work with no
proof coming out of it is just free work.

---

## Delivery flow (keep it identical every time)

| Step | Action | Owner | Time |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | Send BAA + one-page intake | You | Day 0 |
| 2 | Practice returns intake + billing export | Practice | ~5 min |
| 3 | Run the diagnostic (denials, A/R, audit risk) | You | 3–5 days |
| 4 | Send the 1–2 page report | You | Day ~5–7 |
| 5 | 20–30 min walkthrough call | Both | Day ~7–10 |
| 6 | Ask for testimonial + case-study permission | You | On the call |
| 7 | Present founding-client pricing for ongoing work | You | Same call |

Keeping steps 3–4 templated means every review looks sharp and takes you less
time as you go.

## One-page intake (send as a form or plain email)

**Practice**
- Clinic name / location:
- Your name & role:
- Best email & phone:
- EHR / billing system:
- Roughly how many claims submitted per month:

**The situation**
- Rough current denial rate (guess is fine):
- Biggest billing frustration right now:
- Who works denials today (you / front desk / outside biller / nobody)?
- Any recent payer audits or requests for records? (Y/N)

**Logistics**
- OK to proceed once a BAA is signed? (Y/N)
- Can you pull a standard billing export (date range: last 6–12 months)? (Y/N)

That's it — I take it from here.

---

## Testimonial request (say this on the walkthrough call)

> "If the review was useful, the one thing that helps me most is a couple honest
> sentences I can share with other Nevada practices — what shape you were in, what
> the review showed, and whether it was worth your time. I'll write a draft you
> can edit and approve, so it's zero work for you. And I can keep the clinic name
> off it entirely if you'd rather."

**Draft-for-approval template** (fill from their results, send for edits):

> "[Your Name] ran a free denial review for our practice and found [$X / a clear
> pattern] in denied claims we hadn't been working. The report was plain-English
> and the fix list was something my front desk could actually act on. Worth every
> minute. — Dr. [Name] / [City] practice"

Always get **written approval** before publishing. If they want anonymity, publish
as "a [City] chiropractic practice."

---

## Case-study permission (get it in writing — a reply email is fine)

> "Do I have your permission to publish an anonymized summary of these results —
> no clinic name, no patient data, just the denial/recovery figures and what we
> fixed — on my website and LinkedIn? You'll see it before it goes live."

Keep the approval email. Never publish PHI or anything patient-identifiable —
anonymized dollar figures and code patterns only.

---

## Turning the free review into a paying client

On the walkthrough call, after the value has landed:

> "This is the baseline. The recoverable dollars we just found don't collect
> themselves — that's the ongoing work: recurring denial reporting, reworking the
> aged A/R, and getting your front desk to own the workflow. As a founding client
> you'd get [standing discount] on that. Want me to put together what that looks
> like for [Clinic]?"

Founding clients should convert at a higher rate than cold prospects — they've
already seen your work and the exact dollars on the table.
